The Foreclosure of Summer

For 223 years, the cities of Orison have purchased gentle seasons by sending their cold somewhere no citizen can see: 137 years into the future.

Nera Voss, a senior notary in the Office of Seasonal Accounts, has spent her life believing that exact records can keep power honest. Then frost blooms from a sealed ledger in a warm room, and a timestamp from Year 360 begins counting in perfect step with her own. On the far side of the Seasonal Continuity Lattice, millions of people are living through the compound winter her present has borrowed against.

Their first message is not a plea.

As Nera opens a narrow return channel to calendar keeper Siv Ren, every mild day acquires a claimant. Farmers warn that abrupt repayment will destroy the harvest. Heatworkers refuse to bear another sacrifice for protected wards. The First Steward insists that concealment prevented famine and civil war. And Nera's own son asks whether his warmth came with a receipt.

To end the debt without turning repair into punishment, two societies separated by 137 years must attempt something Orison's founders never imagined: share one season as equals.

The Foreclosure of Summer is an original science-fiction novel about climate, inherited obligation, and the moment the future gains the right to answer back.
