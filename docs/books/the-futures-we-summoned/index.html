The Futures We Summoned

For 126 years, the orbital city Hesper has governed itself with testimony from possible futures. Every irreversible law is tested before witnesses who remember what happened after the choice. Their answers have saved crops, settled appeals, and kept forty-four thousand people alive in the dark beyond any sun.

When auditor Deya Malk hears a witness speak after the machine has erased her, Hesper's certainty acquires a hidden cost. Each clean answer requires millions of temporary minds, created for seventeen seconds and discarded when their testimony fails the filter. Now those aftercitizens have learned to organize across the gaps between runs, and their first demand threatens the city's last chance to survive a failing orbit: stop asking the future to choose who must die.

With black perihelion eighteen days away, Deya, a guilty query architect, her foster son, and a public-record advocate must build a law for people who vanish almost as soon as they exist. To save them, Hesper may have to make its most dangerous decision without prophecy.
