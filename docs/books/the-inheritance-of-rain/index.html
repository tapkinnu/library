T. K. Arven

The Inheritance of Rain

On the ocean planet Pelagos, every city drinks from weather shaped by Morrow, an atmospheric lattice trained on the donated memories of the first settlers. Rainfall auditor Tala Rook expects malfunction when Catchment Nine delivers too much water. Instead, a coded droplet gives her a stranger's embodied memory and repeats the private nine-breath ritual her mother taught her during a childhood drought.

The voices inside Morrow are not recordings. They are reconstructing persons whose labor has been inherited as public infrastructure, including Rain-17, a continuity built partly from Tala's mother and seventy years of new experience. Freeing them too quickly could take water from millions. Leaving them in place would preserve a system founded on consent that no longer exists. With a storm diver, an impatient public advocate, and the commissioner who concealed the truth, Tala must build a form of freedom that can survive thirst, incompatible choices, and the right of some rain persons to remain in the sky.
