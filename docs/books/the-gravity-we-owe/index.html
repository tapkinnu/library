The Gravity We Owe

Forty-six habitats survive by sharing twelve captured primordial black holes. The sinks supply their power, steady their orbits, and anchor an economy built on borrowed gravity. Then Aster Three refuses a routine transfer and turns an ordinary handoff into a collision course.

Tug captain Ada Narang discovers the failure reaches across the entire Ophion system. The twelve sinks are not machines and not twelve separate beings. Together they form a distributed alien mind whose thoughts unfold through mass, interval, and orbital recurrence. Humanity has spent ninety years tearing that mind apart and calling the pieces infrastructure.

Restoring it will remove the power that keeps six million people alive. Recontainment may destroy it and the habitats with it. As the orbital web begins to fail, Ada must persuade rival stations to attempt a system-wide migration no central authority can command, while learning that survival may depend on surrendering the control she has trusted since childhood.
