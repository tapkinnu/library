A science fiction novel by Tapio Kinnunen

The Census of Static

On the floating sea-city of Tarn, the census is sacred work: to be counted is to exist. Every quarter, Vey Corwin tallies the living against the slow, corrupted broadcast called the Static — the only thread linking a scattered human diaspora across the Spill. But a set of names keeps arriving that no archive can place: nineteen phantom entries, counted with quiet precision by a people who should not be there at all.

Vey's inquiry uncovers an erasure decades old. The Anver, an aquatic people who once held the waters now settled by humans, were deliberately written out of the census by the Concordance that governs the Spill. Recounting them is forbidden, because their founding claim over human-settled seas could drown a hundred worlds in lawful demands. As Vey builds a shadow census with a handful of dissenting Tellers, she risks war, imprisonment, and the reckoning her own supervisor has spent a career postponing.

The Census of Static is a novel about who counts as a person, the quiet violence of records that decide who is real, and the stubborn courage of a clerk who would rather be wrong with the truth than safe with a lie.
