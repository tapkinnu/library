The Census of Static

On the floating sea-city of Tarn, to be counted is to exist. Census-taker Vey Corwin measures the living against the Static, a damaged broadcast linking the human settlements of the Spill. Then nineteen names begin arriving with perfect regularity—entries no archive can place and no official wants investigated.

The names lead Vey toward an erasure older than the city: an aquatic people removed from the record because acknowledging them would reopen every human claim to the settled seas. Building a true census could trigger war, imprisonment, and the collapse of lawful certainty. Refusing would make Vey complicit in deciding, by omission, who is real.
