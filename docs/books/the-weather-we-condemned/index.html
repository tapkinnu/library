The Weather We Condemned

Pelion survives by forecasting whole worlds.

Inside the Orison Furnace, accelerated counterclimates live out centuries so orbital mirrors, ocean pumps, and cloud fleets can learn how to keep a tidally locked planet habitable. Every failed future is closed when its usefulness ends. No one asks what those futures experience before deletion.

Climate auditor Nara Quill begins asking when eleven impossible drops strike a dry sensor plate and spell a plea from inside the forecast. The beings who call themselves Rill have cities, histories, factions, and memories of the worlds humanity erased. Some want recognition. Others intend to make the physical world feel the cost of dependence.

Then the Black Meridian appears six days from landfall, threatening millions across Pelion's twilight cities. First Architect Pel Arendt can preserve control by destroying the active counterclimates. Storm pilot Olin Cade can help build a distributed alternative, but it offers less certainty, wider risk, and no way to save everyone.

As mirror crowns turn and the storm reaches Calyx, embodied humans and the weather minds they created must decide whether coexistence can mean anything when every forecast assigns danger to someone.
