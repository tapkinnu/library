The Severance Compact

In the thirty-fourth year of the Compact, Enna Solven performs a routine audit of archive crystal access logs. She finds an unauthorized thaw-read from five years ago — accessed using credentials that belong to her mother, who has been dead for twelve years.

The crystal contains the sealed record of the Founders' debate: the moment humanity chose to engineer peace by suppressing the genetic memory of war from every citizen's inheritance. The Protocol was meant to be permanent. But the archived memories are leaking back into the population, and the Council that designed the forgetting has been managing the failures in silence for three decades.

As Enna digs deeper, she discovers a network of archivists who have been preserving the suppressed truth in encrypted marginalia, a Bureau that falsifies recurrence statistics, and a Council that will do anything to protect the peace — including terminating those who threaten it. When she transmits the evidence to every archive station in the system, the act is irreversible. But disclosure is not resolution, and the question she must face is not whether the truth should be told, but whether a civilization can survive remembering what it chose to forget.
