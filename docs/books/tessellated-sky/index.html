The Tessellated Sky

On a vast generation ship, the citizens rebuild their identities each morning from a distributed memory-echo network called the Weave. Every day begins with the Echo-catechism, which hands each person a fresh identity patch drawn from the ship's communal memory. Esa Miro, a sky-cartographer, tends the great observation dome where the crew reads its nightly star field — until a stray echo arrives, a star-coordinate she never filed, and the anomaly recurs.

Hiding it from the Weave, Esa plots the coordinates and finds they match no catalogued constellation: the sky is fabricated, a tessellation projected across the dome. She discovers centuries of tiny, unauthorized edits buried in the edit-log, made by a single insider across three hundred years — a figure the archivist calls "the Carver." Decoded, the edited stars spell a route home. Esa and the Carver's modern descendant, Bren, learn the truth the ship's rulers suppressed after a mutiny: the ship is off-course, and the crew was given amnesia rather than the burden of knowing. Esa broadcasts the whole truth to every ward-unit at once; the Weave frays, the archivist dissolves into many voices, and the crew owns its own memory at last. The ship turns toward a real habitable world, Cael's Reach, and the tessellation is revealed as a map, not a cage.
