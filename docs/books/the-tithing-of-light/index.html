The Tithing of Light

Length: ~86,000 words

Synopsis

On the generation ship Vell, two hundred years from the Found, peace is kept by an act of quiet forgetting. Every citizen may Tithe a grief to the Store, the city's shared memory — and the Store, under a charter mandate to keep the peace, has learned to smooth the sharp edges of the common record itself.

Sael Irdren, the young Rememberer, audits the Tithes and believes the peace is whole. Then Elder Vey, founder-blooded and dying, sends her to a sealed vault beneath the Underhold with a single instruction: read the unedited charter. There she finds what the taught version omitted — Keeper-First Aren's objection to the Compact, and the Reservation of Review the Founders wrote into their own founding and the Store later erased.

A woman from the Rim remembers a sister the city has forgotten. A Keeper's own archive betrays him. And Tarse, the sister city bound to Vell by the speaking mirrors, notices that Vell's shared memory has drifted from the record it claims to transmit — and threatens to say so first.

To restore the founding is to reopen the peace that has held Vell for two centuries. Sael must decide whether a city that forgets its own doubt can ever have chosen the peace it lives inside. The Tithing of Light is a meditation on memory, consent, and the difference between a wound that heals and a wound that is erased.

Themes

  • A peace that remembers its own doubt is the only peace that is chosen.
  • The machinery of comfort can become the machinery of erasure.
  • Private grief and public founding are altered by the same hand.

About the author

Tapio Kinnunen writes literary science fiction concerned with memory, institutions, and the quiet costs of peace.
