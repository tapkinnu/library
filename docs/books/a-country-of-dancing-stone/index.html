A Country of Dancing Stone

Across Neme, roads do not stay where empires put them. Mountains cross valleys in the night, villages follow walking orchards, and licensed ground-dancers force stone to hold still by taking its motion into their own bones.

Tavra Ilen once stood at the center of those measures. Twelve years after an imperial choreography saved a road and buried a district, she cuts work shoes under a false name while the stone in her knees slowly hardens. Then the Imperial College announces the Dominion Measure: one dance to arrest an entire continent forever. Its chosen center is Eda, the daughter Tavra abandoned after the disaster.

With a deserter drummer and a pass-runner from a country that refuses fixed borders, Tavra crosses roads that kick sideways, a bridge performed in rain, and mountains moving against her route. The evidence beneath her feet is worse than she feared. Permanent stillness will not only kill the dancers at the center. It will fossilize springs, roots, and every culture that survives by moving with the land.

But Eda is not waiting to be rescued. She believes mastery can make the Measure safe, and the man who trained her has spent a lifetime preparing to prove it. To stop him, mother and daughter must invent a dance no empire can own, then decide who enters its center, who may leave, and whether a stable world is worth the bodies required to hold it.
