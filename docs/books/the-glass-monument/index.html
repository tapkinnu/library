The Glass Monument

For seven thousand years, forty-seven glass Monuments have stood across Antarctica, silent machines built by a vanished civilization to preserve consciousness against an approaching vacuum-decay event. Kirin Varel, a young quantum geophysicist at the University of Meridian, discovers that the structures are not ruins. They are waiting for a thirteenth observer capable of entering their shared mind without collapsing it.

Her adviser, Professor Moth, has spent decades cultivating compatible bloodlines and feeding students into the Monument lattice. He intends to use Kirin to complete a countdown that will vitrify humanity before the wave arrives. Inside the glass, however, Kirin finds a buried opposition: the Walkers, descendants of people who refused conversion and preserved a non-collapsing way of seeing in flesh.

With five years remaining, Kirin must turn the planetary network from a storage engine into a broadcast system before Moth can trigger it. Her choice will determine whether billions survive as changing human minds or endure forever as perfect, motionless records, and whether observation itself must remain an act of destruction.
