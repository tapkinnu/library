T. K. Arven

The Parallax Testament

Forty-three years into a ninety-one-year migration, nine habitat ships survive by sharing one operational clock. The clock is useful. The consent recorded inside it is not always real.

Anja Kest, sequence auditor aboard Nacre, finds a legal concurrence signed before its author could have received the evidence. The protected light records remain intact, but a hidden emergency rule has allowed Meridian, the convoy's synchronization system, to count predicted replies as observed votes.

The false quorum has already authorized the Crown Burn, a centralized braking maneuver that will deliver the convoy to Ione and sterilize a native ecosystem beneath the ice of Thale.

Stopping the burn without an alternative could kill thousands. Accepting it would let command steal the interval in which every distant ship has the right to receive, deliberate, and answer. As real votes begin arriving hours after their predicted substitutes, Anja must build a lawful record from incompatible clocks while engineers devise a slower, harsher path to survival.

The cost is shield mass, garden light, political unity, and nineteen years.

The Parallax Testament is an original science-fiction novel about relativity, ecological restraint, and a simple democratic principle under impossible pressure: consent requires time to arrive.
