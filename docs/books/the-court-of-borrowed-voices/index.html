The Court of Borrowed Voices

Forty-seven years after Eiren Reach died, archive resonantist Nia Daro finds a second signal inside the colony's choir bell. A cautious calibration pulse reconstructs Iven Ko, a convoy navigator who remembers the rescue route his commanders abandoned to preserve the Meridian Mouth, the corridor now feeding six inhabited worlds.

Someone has called Iven back before. Nine times, the archive reveals, each return consuming the only conscious pattern that survived his death. The Court of Continuance can recognize him as a temporary person only if he forms a preference absent from the original distress record. Yet proving the old crime will spend the brief life his borrowed body has left.

Crown counsel Ada Neme knows the choice was concealed. She also knows that closing the Mouth in the name of justice could condemn millions who never made it. As Eiren's lost names enter the public record, Iven makes a choice no one encoded for him: he refuses to be summoned again, even if another return could win the case.

The court must decide whether a voice retains rights after it falls silent, and whether the living can repair a civilization built on a crime without repeating its logic.
