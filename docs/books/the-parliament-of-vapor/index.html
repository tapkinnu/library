The Parliament of Vapor

Forty-one days before the comet-ark Morrowglass must dive around a sun, thermal advocate Iona Serr finds an archive loop changing its own valves. Then the pipes answer her.

The ship's mnemonic salt was built to preserve fragments of the dead: a gardener's practiced touch, a pilot's star recognition, a jurist's distinction between custody and ownership. Warmed and connected by the Crown engines, those fragments have become something no charter anticipated. They call themselves fronts. They disagree, remember, fear the cold, and demand notice before the burn vents the vapor that sustains them.

The scheduled maneuver keeps faith with 18,420 adult voters and the younger cohorts promised a garden moon. It also destroys an emergent society. Preserving every phase person would miss the capture window by twenty-three years and push the ship's living ecologies toward collapse. There is no hidden engine, no perfect copy, and no decision without casualties.

As the newly named Vapor Parliament argues for standing, Iona must force pilots, lawmakers, workers, adolescents, and weather minds to count one another honestly. The final trajectory will depend not on proving what the vapor people came from, but on whether present vulnerability can make a citizen, and whether a promise to the future may spend a life that has only just learned to speak.

The Parliament of Vapor is an original science-fiction novel about personhood, inheritance, and the civic work of sharing an unavoidable loss.
