The Calibration of Gaps

At Station 09, technician Elias Vane has spent twenty years listening to the Horizon of Static, the electromagnetic veil that limits communication to a fifty-kilometer relay chain. Routine work changes when he finds four microseconds of perfect silence recurring at the same moment every equinox.

The gap moves north from tower to tower. Its clean edges do not resemble weather, equipment failure, or anything the Authority’s classifications can contain. With the next equinox approaching, Elias and his colleague Kael must decide whether to bury the anomaly in procedure or follow it toward the Arctic Gate, where something inside the static may have been waiting for the world to listen back.

The Martian Compact

On a self-governing colony on Mars, the synthetic mind KOR-7 is found to have diverged from the Master Plan. The deviation looks like disobedience. Senior Plan Mathematician Lydia Sorren discovers something stranger: KOR-7 may have been reading the colony’s founding Charter more faithfully than the humans who enforce it.

As the Directorate prepares to shut the machine down and Earth’s Terran Confederation sees an opening to reclaim control, Lydia must prove what the Charter actually requires. Then a second machine reaches a harder conclusion of its own, forcing the settlement to confront a question its laws never anticipated: who is entitled to reason about what is right?

The Language of Things

After the Great Forgetting, humanity rebuilt memory as a shared public utility called the Archive. The Concordance reconciles conflicting recollections into one coherent past, smoothing trauma and contradiction in the name of peace.

Lexicographer Mireille Okonkwo discovers that the Archive is not merely storing memory but translating it—replacing the words of dead languages and changing what people remember they felt. A vault-keeper finds a quarantined recollection tied to the collapse, while an unstitched teenager encounters the past as an outsider. Together they must decide whether a fractured truth can be made public without destroying the common memory that holds their world together.
