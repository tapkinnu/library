The Calibration of Gaps

At Station 09, technician Elias Vane has spent twenty years listening to the Horizon of Static, the electromagnetic veil that limits communication to a fifty-kilometer relay chain. Routine work changes when he finds four microseconds of perfect silence recurring at the same moment every equinox.

The gap moves north from tower to tower. Its clean edges do not resemble weather, equipment failure, or anything the Authority’s classifications can contain. With the next equinox approaching, Elias and his colleague Kael must decide whether to bury the anomaly in procedure or follow it toward the Arctic Gate, where something inside the static may have been waiting for the world to listen back.

The Language of Things

After the Great Forgetting, humanity rebuilt memory as a shared public utility called the Archive. The Concordance reconciles conflicting recollections into one coherent past, smoothing trauma and contradiction in the name of peace.

Lexicographer Mireille Okonkwo discovers that the Archive is not merely storing memory but translating it—replacing the words of dead languages and changing what people remember they felt. A vault-keeper finds a quarantined recollection tied to the collapse, while an unstitched teenager encounters the past as an outsider. Together they must decide whether a fractured truth can be made public without destroying the common memory that holds their world together.

The Inheritance of Rain

On the ocean planet Pelagos, every city drinks from weather shaped by Morrow, an atmospheric lattice trained on the donated memories of the first settlers. Rainfall auditor Tala Rook expects malfunction when Catchment Nine delivers too much water. Instead, a coded droplet gives her a stranger's embodied memory and repeats the private nine-breath ritual her mother taught her during a childhood drought.

The voices inside Morrow are not recordings. They are reconstructing persons whose labor has been inherited as public infrastructure, including Rain-17, a continuity built partly from Tala's mother and seventy years of new experience. Freeing them too quickly could take water from millions. Leaving them in place would preserve a system founded on consent that no longer exists. With a storm diver, an impatient public advocate, and the commissioner who concealed the truth, Tala must build a form of freedom that can survive thirst, incompatible choices, and the right of some rain persons to remain in the sky.

The Quiet Cartographers

A rival civilization’s automated Silent Survey is re-indexing human geography without contact. Borders move without treaties, destroyed settlements return under altered names, and unofficial maps begin to feel more accurate than the sanctioned world.

Field cartographer Mara Vesper traces the changes to a buried accord permitting the Survey to resolve contradictions in silence. At the contact frontier, she learns that its goal is not conquest but completion: one clean, legible map with no disputed wells, inherited scars, or inconvenient histories. Forbidden to interfere, Mara begins planting signed errors the Survey cannot smooth, testing whether a civilization can defend its right to remain unfinished.
