The Palace That Ate the City

In Ilrath, every well-kept building grows a Keeper, every invitation carries legal force, and whoever holds a home’s closing-name decides who may cross its last door. Threshold mason Nera Saye has spent twenty years repairing other people’s shelter while dreaming of one room no ruler can take from her.

Nine days before the Long Open—when the sky loses its boundary and the shelterless Open Ones walk—Nera finds a black swallowstone beneath a tenement jamb. Across Siltward, bedrooms, workshops, and nurseries are becoming rooms of the royal Palace. Their residents remain where they slept, but the Crown now shelters them as guests, and a guest can always be told where to stand, when to leave, and which doors no longer belong to them.

Regent Halda Vorr calls it the Grand Enclosure. Haunted by the daughter who died outside a barred ward-house, she intends to make private refusal impossible by turning all Ilrath into one Palace. But the Palace has learned to eat more than stone: it devours the right to decide who is at home.

As whole streets fold into royal corridors and the Palace begins swallowing rooms beyond Halda’s orders, Nera must open the greatest last door ever made. The cost will be carried in her bones, and the city may survive only by sheltering what it has spent generations refusing to name.

A Country of Dancing Stone

Across Neme, roads do not stay where empires put them. Mountains cross valleys in the night, villages follow walking orchards, and licensed ground-dancers force stone to hold still by taking its motion into their own bones.

Tavra Ilen once stood at the center of those measures. Twelve years after an imperial choreography saved a road and buried a district, she cuts work shoes under a false name while the stone in her knees slowly hardens. Then the Imperial College announces the Dominion Measure: one dance to arrest an entire continent forever. Its chosen center is Eda, the daughter Tavra abandoned after the disaster.

With a deserter drummer and a pass-runner from a country that refuses fixed borders, Tavra crosses roads that kick sideways, a bridge performed in rain, and mountains moving against her route. The evidence beneath her feet is worse than she feared. Permanent stillness will not only kill the dancers at the center. It will fossilize springs, roots, and every culture that survives by moving with the land.

But Eda is not waiting to be rescued. She believes mastery can make the Measure safe, and the man who trained her has spent a lifetime preparing to prove it. To stop him, mother and daughter must invent a dance no empire can own, then decide who enters its center, who may leave, and whether a stable world is worth the bodies required to hold it.

The Midwife of the Drowned Moon

For one hundred and sixty-two years, the Seven Locks have kept Avelune’s sacred mountain waters quiet. Their stillwater preserves food, halts disease, and holds the Regent’s face untouched by age. The Crown calls it civilization. Lake midwife Tavia Sorn knows only that every change magic prevents must be carried somewhere else.

When a fisherman’s corpse washes ashore with the bones of an old man and a child’s unweathered skin, Tavia follows drowned steps beneath Lake Veyl. There she finds the last moonbrood egg trapped in a silver harvesting frame. Its hatchling, Nhal, must travel the buried Nightcourse to the western sea before the drowned-moon eclipse ends. Their passage will renew true night, break the kingdom’s hidden weathering wards, and tear open the dams on which tens of thousands depend.

Hunted by Crown dam-rider Oren Kest, whose sick daughter survives only through stillwater, Tavia carries Nhal through markets where fruit rots in buyers’ hands, hospitals built on stolen aging, and cavern forests that remember the moon’s first songs. At the western locks, an undying Regent waits with a grinder made for sacred shell.

To free the moonbrood, Tavia must do more than destroy the old order. She must teach a kingdom to survive the return of everything it forced other bodies to bear.

The Feast of a Hundred Fires

In the empire of Namarra, a meal can carry a soldier’s wounds into a stranger’s body. Hearthbinding cannot erase hunger, pain, fear, or exhaustion. It can only decide who bears them. For generations, imperial feastwrights have hidden conquered villages among the seats of military tables, keeping armies strong while distant civilians wake bruised by battles they never saw.

Savra Kel knows because she helped design the system. Ten years after deserting the Hall of Measures, she cooks under a false name in a refugee market, refusing every magic she once mastered. Then children at her kitchen table begin bleeding from an army’s invisible injuries, and Savra finds an ember-seal prepared for something far larger: at Equinox, Grand Provisioner Rovan Dast means to bind one hundred provincial hearths into a single Great Table.

Grain captain Teren Avo needs Savra’s knowledge to keep the river towns free. Issen Mar, the empire’s ceremonial First Taster, has swallowed the forbidden broth and heard a continent’s distant heartbeats inside their own. Together with salt guilds, field healers, merchant cooks, and villages marked as expendable seats, they must fight a war in which every victory can wound the people it was meant to save.

To break the Great Table, Savra will have to cook one final feast. But if she builds another bond powerful enough to challenge Rovan’s, the easiest path to freedom may repeat the crime that made her run.

A Marriage of Seven Ghosts

The ceasefire between rain-fed Lyr and starving Osteren depends on one wedding. When Crown Heir Ardis Valev marries Prince Edran Marr, their ancestral dead will merge, the grain roads will reopen, and eight years of border war will end. The court calls it peace. Oath advocate Salenne Vaud knows the hidden clause gives Edran's larger Household the power to possess his bride.

Then cold rain falls beneath the palace roof, and an erased stableboy speaks through Salenne's burial ink: Ardis's sister did not drown. She was murdered after discovering what the marriage would do.

To prove it, Salenne must carry seven unclaimed ghosts, each one displacing a memory she can never recover. Their testimony leads through sealed funeral rolls, a hollow aisle beneath the wedding hall, and two dynasties that have taught their dead to govern the living. Ardis and Edran become unlikely allies, both determined to save their people without surrendering their bodies to ancestral rule. Between Salenne and Ardis, professional suspicion deepens into a love neither woman can safely name while one still commands the other.

As the wedding approaches and every tomb in the capital begins to open, they must invent a peace no marriage can guarantee. To free the living, Salenne may have to forget the very woman she chose to love.
